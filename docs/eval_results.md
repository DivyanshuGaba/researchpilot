# Evaluation results

Generated by `python -m eval.report` from `backend/eval/results/*.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Setup

49 questions (41 answerable, 8 unanswerable) over 4 open-access arXiv papers, dataset `5f0e9de403bf8459`, embedding `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, reranker `cross_encoder`, answers by `claude-sonnet-5-5`, judge `claude-opus-5-5`, confidence threshold 0.05.

- **Corpus:** *Attention Is All You Need*, *BERT*, *Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks*, *Lost in the Middle* (arXiv, pinned by SHA-256 in `eval/docs.json`; fetch with `python -m eval.fetch_docs`).
- **Questions:** hand written against the text. Every answer carries a verbatim evidence phrase that `eval/verify_dataset.py` checks appears on the stated page, and the unanswerable questions' key terms are checked to be absent. Types: factual, multi-hop, table, cross-document comparison, unanswerable (5 off-topic, 3 near-miss).
- **Chunking size** is the section (parent) size sent to the model; the searched children are a quarter of it (`400/100`, `800/200`, `1200/300` tokens).
- **Retrieval metrics** are page-level: a hit is a retrieved chunk whose page range overlaps a gold page of the right document. `recall@k` is the share of gold pages covered by the top k, `MRR` the reciprocal rank of the first hit. They use the reranked, diversified top 10 before the confidence gate.
- **Generation metrics** come from an LLM judge that sees the sources the model saw: *faithfulness* is the share of claims supported by those sources (refusals excluded), *correctness* scores correct/partial/incorrect as 1/0.5/0 against the reference (a refusal of an answerable question counts 0), *relevance* is 1-5 scaled to 0-1, *citation precision* is the share of `[c_N]` markers whose source supports the claim. *Refusal accuracy* is the share of unanswerable questions refused; *false refusal rate* the share of answerable ones refused.

## How much to trust a difference

With 41 answerable questions one question is worth 0.024, and the standard error of a proportion near 0.85 is about 0.056. **Differences smaller than roughly 0.05 to 0.1 between two rows are within noise.** Treat the tables as ranking evidence, not proof, and re-run on a larger set before changing a default for a small gain.
